Nostalgia

Nostalgia is a feeling often stirred by the quiet, reflective journeys of characters who are searching for pieces of themselves. As they uncover forgotten memories or hidden truths, a sense of longing begins to surface—not just in the characters, but in the reader too. The past becomes something hazy and unreachable, yet achingly present through the author’s delicate and introspective language.In Japanese literature, nostalgia often unfolds in small, intimate moments—through a familiar scent, a fleeting memory, or a place that no longer exists. These stories don’t just evoke the past; they invite the reader to feel the weight of time, loss, and unspoken yearning alongside the characters.

A Hundred Years and a Day: 34 Stories


Written by Tomoka Shibasaki

Translated by Polly Barton

Synopsis

A Hundred Years and a Day: 34 Stories is set in a contemporary world marked by disconnection, these stories explore fleeting yet meaningful moments of human connection. People cross paths, share fragments of their lives, and eventually drift apart. We encounter women who live together during wartime before parting ways, a man moving from one rooftop apartment to the next, two brothers growing up alone while their parents work in factories, and a ramen shop that somehow survives even as everything around it disappears. In another story, strangers gather to witness a new kind of spaceship take off from a former island resort pier—and that’s just the beginning. Spanning 34 short tales from across the globe, this collection reads with the immediacy of breaking news, told in a spare yet symbolic style that lingers with the reader.(Good Reads).
