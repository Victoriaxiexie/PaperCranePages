Thought-provoking

Thought-provoking books entail stories which spark reflection, raise questions, and quietly challenge the way we see the world. They don’t always offer clear answers but that’s part of what makes them powerful.

Days at the Morisaki Bookshop


Written by Mieko Kawakami

Translated by Sam Bett and David Boyd

Synopsis

Tucked away in Jimbocho, Tokyo’s famed book district, sits a cozy second-hand bookshop nestled inside an old wooden building. It’s been in Takako’s family for generations, though she’s never had much interest in reading. Her uncle Satoru, however, has dedicated his life to the shop, especially since his wife left him five years ago. When Takako's relationship falls apart, she reluctantly moves into the small room above the bookstore, rent-free hoping for a quiet place to heal. But surrounded by shelves of well-worn pages and the steady presence of her uncle, she begins to uncover a world she never expected. As the seasons change, so does her perspective. In the heart of the Morisaki bookshop, Takako and Satoru learn that stories and the people who love them can bring comfort, understanding, and the courage to begin again(Good Reads).
