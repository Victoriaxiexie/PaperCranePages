Science Fiction

Science Fiction is a genre that dares to ask, what if? It explores the ripple effects of impossible or improbable shifts in reality. For instance, through time travel, alien encounters, or environmental collapse. These stories imagine alternate worlds and futures, using elements of fantasy, technology, or biology to reflect on the human condition and sometimes the non-human too (Oxford Reference). In Japanese literature, sci-fi often carries a distinct tone—quiet, philosophical, and richly human. Whether it’s questioning the cost of progress or pondering life after humanity, these stories are as emotionally resonant as they are speculative.

Klara and The Sun

Written by Kazuo Ishiguro

Synopsis

Klara and the Sun follows the story of Klara, an Artificial Friend designed to observe and understand human behavior. From her place in a shop, she watches hopeful customers and strangers, waiting for the day someone will choose her. Through Klara’s unique perspective, the novel offers a moving exploration of connection and longing as we learn about the humans in her life. This story seeks to truly ask what it means to love in an ever-evolving world. (Good Reads).
