Literary Fiction

Literary fiction invites readers to slow down and sink into stories that are less about plot twists and more about meaning. These works focus on language, character, and emotion that offer reflections on loss, identity and everything in between. Often subtle and deeply human, literary fiction asks not just what happens, but why it matters.

Convenience Store Woman


Written by Sayaka Murata

Translated by Ginny Tapley Takemori

Synopsis

Convenience Store Woman is the acclaimed English-language debut of one of Japan’s most celebrated contemporary authors, telling the unexpected and touching story of Keiko Furukura, a 36-year-old woman living in Tokyo. Keiko has always struggled to fit in at home, in school, and in society. But everything changes when she starts working at the “Smile Mart” convenience store at eighteen. There, she finally finds a sense of order and belonging, guided by the store's detailed manual and mimicking her coworkers to blend in. Keiko feels fulfilled and content in her routine but those around her don’t understand. Pressured by family and peers to settle down and pursue a more “normal” life, Keiko makes a decision that challenges their expectations. With sharp insight and quiet humor, this novel offers a fresh take on individuality, societal norms, and the quiet rebellion of choosing your own path (Good Reads).
